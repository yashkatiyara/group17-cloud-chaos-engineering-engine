📘 Glossary — plain-English definitions of every term on this page
Chaos Engineering
Breaking your own system on purpose, in a controlled way, to find weaknesses before a real outage finds them for you.
Fault Injection
The actual act of causing a failure — killing a container, slowing the network, dropping packets, maxing out CPU.
Steady State
How the system behaves when everything is healthy — the "normal" baseline you compare against during an experiment.
SLO (Service Level Objective)
A safety line you decide in advance, e.g. "error rate must stay under 15%." Crossing it means the experiment has gone too far.
P95 Latency
95% of requests are faster than this number. A better "worst-case for most users" measure than a simple average.
Circuit Breaker
Software fuse box. Watches live health metrics and automatically disconnects/undoes a fault the instant it becomes dangerous.
Blast Radius
How much of the system gets damaged by a fault. Good chaos engineering keeps this small and contained.
Rollback
Undoing the fault: restarting a killed container, clearing a network-delay rule, flushing a firewall-drop rule, killing stress processes.
Docker Container
A lightweight, isolated box that runs one program (here: one microservice) with its own filesystem and network view.
